---
title: UNC-CH AAUP Letter to the Board of Trustees
---

Ahead of the Board of Trustees meeting on November 11–12, 2026, the
UNC-Chapel Hill chapter of the AAUP sent a letter to the members of
the Board. We are heartened by the midterm election results, not
because we want one political party to intervene in academic affairs
instead of another, but because we hope they will end all
interference by politicians and other outside interests in the
policies and curricula on our campus.

We call on each Trustee to endorse the following necessary actions:

1. Commit to campus policies conceived through faculty governance,
   produced in dialogue with our Chancellor and other campus
   administrators;
2. Defund the
   [scandal-ridden](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/education/higher-education/unc-chapel-hill-investigates-scill/)
   and [political
   boondoggle](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/education/higher-education/north-carolina-budget-higher-ed-spending/)
   School of Civic Life and Leadership (SCiLL);
3. End [false austerity
   measures](https://dailytarheel.com/249902/university/university-budget-aaup-audit-event/)
   (such as Service First) that have led to proposed cuts to staffing
   and graduate student funding amidst student enrollment increases
   and other revenue growth;
4. Confirm that accreditation will be a non-governmental, apolitical
   process, which is not the case with the proposed [Commission on
   Public Higher
   Education](https://www.aaup.org/news/new-cphe-accrediting-body-illegitimate);
   and
5. End
   [interference](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/education/higher-education/unc-trustees-reject-hiring-tenured-womens-studies-professor/)
   in faculty hiring.

Last summer, [an article in *The News and
Observer*](https://www.newsobserver.com/latest-news/article316711353.html)
reported that at least three Trustees had expressed concerns that “the
board meddles in matters beyond its purview.” We agree, and we urge
those members to make their concerns known and to speak to these five
critical points at this week’s meeting. As one Trustee wrote, “Reform
is needed to make sure future boards are not overly influenced by
outside political forces.” The time to undo this political
interference, and the damage it has caused, begins now.
